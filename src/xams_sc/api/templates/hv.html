The high-voltage columns, and what this page cannot do

Status comes from the board's own STAT word, never from the voltage. There are four states, and three separate things decide them:

  • disabled — the front-panel enable switch is off. Nothing in this software can change that, by design (§10a).
  • enabled — the switch is on, but the output is not energised. Permitted and inert: it will do nothing until it is switched on, which is a command, not the switch.
  • ON 0 V — energised and sitting at zero. Live, with nothing on it yet.
  • ON — energised with volts out.

A trip, interlock or over-current replaces all four and is shown in red. The distinction between disabled and enabled is the one that matters: flipping the switch does not energise anything, and for a while this page wrongly showed both as "off".

VMON and IMON are what the supply is measuring now. Voltages are stored signed: the supplies report unsigned magnitudes with polarity as a separate parameter, so the sign is applied in software and the cathode reads negative while the anode reads positive (§7.2). A supply that is off reads 0 and is shown as off, not as a fault.

MAXV, RUP, RDW, TRIP and POL are the board's own protection settings, shown from devices.yaml. The software displays them and raises an alarm if the board disagrees with what is recorded — it cannot change them (§8.3). Ramp rate, trip current and over-voltage limit live on the instrument, and that is what keeps this software out of the protection path (§10).

VSET is what the channel was told to hold; VMON is what it is doing. VSET is written from the boxes above, read back from the board and audited; VMON is only ever read.

A VSET in red is a disabled channel whose setpoint is not zero. The enable switch is a hand operation and the board ramps to VSET the moment it is flipped, so a red value is the voltage you would get by touching that switch - with no confirmation and no warning. §10a turns "a disabled channel has VSET 0" into an invariant precisely so that the switch becomes safe to flip; until the setpoints are zeroed it is not.

What this page cannot do, by design. It cannot flip a channel's enable switch, and it cannot take a board out of LOCAL - both are hand operations at the supply, and they are the two gates between this software and an electrode (§10a). It cannot change MAXV, RUP, RDW, TRIP or ISET. And it never acts on its own: a restart writes nothing.

The two units are told apart by asking each board for its own serial number over the wire, never by COM port. Swapping the USB cables is therefore harmless (§6.2).
